Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number that estimates body fat based on height and weight. In this guide, you'll learn the formula, how to interpret results, and when BMI can be misleading.

The BMI Formula

For metric units:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)

Example: 70 kg and 1.75 m tall → 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9 (Normal).

BMI Categories

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5–24.9Normal
25–29.9Overweight
30+Obese

Why BMI Isn't Everything

BMI doesn't distinguish muscle from fat. Athletes may have high BMI but low body fat. Use it as a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

FAQ

Is BMI accurate for children?

No — children use age- and sex-specific percentiles.

What's a healthy BMI?

18.5–24.9 is considered normal for most adults.
